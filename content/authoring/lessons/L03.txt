@lesson matatu-to-town
title: Matatu to Town
level: 3
theme: transport
category: transport
culture: Matatus are Nairobi's privately run minibuses — loud music, graffiti art, and a makanga (conductor) hanging out of the door shouting the route. Fares aren't fixed: they rise at rush hour and when it rains, so it's normal to ask "Nauli ni ngapi?" before you get in. You get off by shouting where you want to stop. Imperatives are the plain verb: Panda! (get in), Shuka! (get off), Simama! (stop).
start: You're at the stage in Kilimani. A makanga is shouting for passengers to town.
grammar: future-ta, imperatives, numbers-money
gl: Kencom = Kencom — the big matatu stop in the CBD
gl: Tao = "Town!" — the makanga's call: this matatu goes to the CBD.
gl: kiti ya mbele = "the front seat" — Kenyan agreement. || Sanifu: kiti cha mbele (kiti is ki/vi) #sanifu-noun-classes
gl: iko na mtu = "is taken" — lit. "is with someone". Kenyan -ko na for "have". || Sanifu: kina mtu #na-have
gl: kawaida = "normally / usually"
gl: Nashuka = "I'm getting off" — short for ninashuka. #present-na
gl: Haya = "Alright!"
gl: benki = bank
gl: rush hour = rush hour (English)
gl: inapanda = "is going up" — kupanda = to climb / go up #present-na

> Makanga: Tao! Tao! Kencom! Panda, boss! | Town! Town! Kencom! Get in, boss!
< Mgeni: Matatu hii inaenda Kencom? | Does this matatu go to Kencom?
  x: Matatu hii anaenda Kencom? ; Matatu hizi inaenda Kencom?
> Makanga: Ndiyo, moja kwa moja. Panda haraka, tunaondoka! | Yes, straight there. Get in quickly, we're leaving!
< Mgeni: Nauli ni ngapi? | How much is the fare?
  x: Nauli ni wapi? ; Nauli ni nani?
> Makanga: Ni mia moja. | It's a hundred.
< Mgeni: Mia moja? Kawaida ni sabini! | A hundred? Normally it's seventy!
  x: Mia moja? Kawaida ni sabini tu, asante! ; Mia moja? Sitaki sabini!
> Makanga: Saa hii ni rush hour, boss. Bei inapanda. | Right now it's rush hour, boss. The price goes up.
< Mgeni: Sawa. Nitalipa mia moja, lakini nipe kiti ya mbele. | Okay. I'll pay a hundred, but give me the front seat.
  s: Sawa. Nitalipa mia moja, lakini nipe kiti cha mbele.
> Makanga: Kiti ya mbele iko na mtu. Kaa hapa nyuma, kuna nafasi. | The front seat is taken. Sit here at the back, there's space.
  s: Kiti cha mbele kina mtu. Kaa hapa nyuma, kuna nafasi.
< Mgeni: Haya. Nitashuka Kencom. Utaniambia? | Alright. I'll get off at Kencom. Will you tell me?
  x: Haya. Utashuka Kencom. Nitakuambia? ; Haya. Nitashuka Kencom. Nitakuambia?
> Makanga: Sawa, nitakuambia. | Okay, I'll tell you.
> Makanga: Kencom! Kencom! Nani anashuka? | Kencom! Kencom! Who's getting off?
< Mgeni: Mimi! Simama hapa, nashuka! | Me! Stop here, I'm getting off!
  x: Mimi! Panda hapa, nashuka! ; Mimi! Simama hapa, anashuka!
> Makanga: Dereva, simama! Shuka haraka, boss. | Driver, stop! Get off quickly, boss.
< Mgeni: Asante! Matatu ya kurudi Kilimani iko wapi? | Thanks! Where's the matatu back to Kilimani?
  x: Asante! Matatu ya kurudi Kilimani ziko wapi? ; Asante! Matatu ya kurudi Kilimani yuko wapi?
> Makanga: Iko pale, karibu na benki. Uliza tu! | It's over there, near the bank. Just ask!

@lesson kibanda-lunch
title: Lunch at the Kibanda
level: 3
theme: food
category: food-drink
culture: A kibanda (or "kibanda cha chakula") is a roadside food stall: plastic chairs, a hot plate, and the best-value lunch in Nairobi — chapati, beans (maharagwe), ugali with meat or sukuma. Tea comes already sweetened unless you say otherwise. To order, Kenyans usually say "Niletee…" (bring me…) or the softer "Naweza kupata…?" (can I get…?). "Karibu chakula" is the polite thing to say when food arrives — lit. "welcome to the food".
start: Lunchtime. You sit down at a kibanda near the office.
grammar: future-ta, imperatives, helper-verbs
gl: Mnapika = "you (all) are cooking" — m- (you all) + -na- + -pika. Asking the staff as a group. #present-na
gl: Karibu chakula = "Enjoy your meal" — lit. "welcome to the food"
gl: kitu ingine = "anything else" — Kenyan all-purpose ingine. || Sanifu: kitu kingine (ki/vi class) #sanifu-noun-classes
gl: kwa ukuta = "on the wall" — Kenyan use of kwa. || Sanifu: ukutani #locative-ni
gl: Chakula itakuja = "The food will come" — Kenyan n/n agreement on chakula. || Sanifu: Chakula kitakuja (ki/vi) #future-ta

> Mhudumu: Karibu! Kaa hapa. Utakula nini? | Welcome! Sit here. What will you eat?
< Mteja: Asante. Mnapika nini leo? | Thanks. What are you cooking today?
  x: Asante. Ninapika nini leo? ; Asante. Mnapika wapi leo?
> Mhudumu: Tuna chapati, maharagwe, ugali na nyama, na sukuma. | We have chapati, beans, ugali with meat, and sukuma.
< Mteja: Naweza kupata chapati mbili na maharagwe? | Can I get two chapatis and beans?
  x: Unaweza kupata chapati mbili na maharagwe? ; Naweza kupata chapati wawili na maharagwe?
> Mhudumu: Ndiyo. Utakunywa chai au soda? | Yes. Will you drink tea or soda?
< Mteja: Niletee chai, tafadhali. Lakini sitaki sukari nyingi. | Bring me tea, please. But I don't want a lot of sugar.
  x: Mletee chai, tafadhali. Lakini sitaki sukari nyingi. ; Niletee chai, tafadhali. Lakini nataka sukari nyingi.
> Mhudumu: Chai hapa inakuja na sukari tayari. | Tea here comes with sugar already.
< Mteja: Aah, sawa. Lete tu. | Ah, okay. Just bring it.
  x: Aah, sawa. Kula tu. ; Aah, sawa. Lipa tu.
> Mhudumu: Haya. Chakula itakuja baada ya dakika tano. | Alright. The food will come in five minutes.
  s: Haya. Chakula kitakuja baada ya dakika tano.
> Mhudumu: Hii hapa. Karibu chakula! | Here it is. Enjoy your meal!
< Mteja: Asante! Inanukia vizuri sana. | Thanks! It smells really good.
  x: Asante! Ninanukia vizuri sana. ; Asante! Inanukia vibaya sana.
> Mhudumu: Utahitaji kitu ingine? | Will you need anything else?
  s: Utahitaji kitu kingine?
< Mteja: Hapana, asante. Nitalipa sasa. Ni pesa ngapi? | No, thanks. I'll pay now. How much is it?
  x: Hapana, asante. Utalipa sasa. Ni pesa ngapi? ; Hapana, asante. Nitalipa sasa. Ni pesa wapi?
> Mhudumu: Ni mia mbili na hamsini. Lipa kwa M-Pesa, till iko kwa ukuta. | It's 250. Pay with M-Pesa — the till number is on the wall.
< Mteja: Sawa. Chakula ni tamu sana! Nitarudi kesho. | Okay. The food is really tasty! I'll come back tomorrow.
  x: Sawa. Chakula ni tamu sana! Nilirudi kesho. ; Sawa. Chakula ni tamu sana! Utarudi kesho.

@lesson saturday-plans
title: Plans for Saturday (and Swahili Time)
level: 3
theme: friends
category: time
culture: Swahili time starts at sunrise, not midnight. 7 a.m. is saa moja ("the first hour"), 8 a.m. is saa mbili, noon is saa sita, and 6 p.m. is saa kumi na mbili. The trick: add or subtract six from the clock time. Kenyans switch between Swahili time and English time ("seven") all day — when it matters, check which one someone means! A day trip to Lake Naivasha (90 km away) is a classic weekend plan.
start: Your friend Otieno calls you on Wednesday evening.
grammar: future-ta, time-swahili, helper-verbs, locative-ni
gl: Sema = "Talk to me!" — casual phone hello (lit. "say!")
gl: seven = "seven" — English time. Kenyans mix English and Swahili time all the time.
gl: ziwani = "at the lake" — ziwa + -ni (place suffix). #locative-ni
gl: Safi sana = "Excellent!" — lit. "very clean"
gl: marafiki = friends (plural of rafiki)
gl: tuondoke = "(that) we leave" — subjunctive after lazima: tu- + -ondok- + -e. #subjunctive
gl: kali = "fierce / strong / sharp" — jua ni kali = the sun is strong
gl: huko = "there" — that place (Naivasha)

> Otieno: Sema, John! Utafanya nini Jumamosi? | Hey, John! What will you do on Saturday?
< John: Sijui bado. Kwa nini? | I don't know yet. Why?
  x: Najua bado. Kwa nini? ; Sijui bado. Kwa wapi?
> Otieno: Tutaenda Naivasha na marafiki. Utakuja? | We're going to Naivasha with friends. Will you come?
< John: Ndiyo, nitakuja! Mtaondoka saa ngapi? | Yes, I'll come! What time will you leave?
  x: Ndiyo, utakuja! Mtaondoka saa ngapi? ; Ndiyo, nitakuja! Mtaondoka saa gani?
> Otieno: Saa moja asubuhi. Lazima tuondoke mapema, kuna foleni. | At seven in the morning. We have to leave early, there's traffic.
< John: Saa moja? Mapema sana! Saa moja ni seven, sivyo? | Saa moja? Very early! Saa moja is seven, right?
  x: Saa moja? Mapema sana! Saa moja ni one, sivyo? ; Saa moja? Mapema sana! Saa moja ni six, sivyo?
> Otieno: Ndiyo, seven. Tutapita kwako saa kumi na mbili na nusu. | Yes, seven. We'll pass by your place at six thirty.
< John: Sawa. Nitakuwa tayari. Nitaleta nini? | Okay. I'll be ready. What should I bring?
  x: Sawa. Utakuwa tayari. Nitaleta nini? ; Sawa. Nitakuwa tayari. Nitaleta wapi?
> Otieno: Lete maji na kofia. Jua ni kali sana huko. | Bring water and a hat. The sun is really strong there.
< John: Poa. Na chakula? | Cool. And food?
  x: Poa. Na chakula wapi? ; Pole. Na chakula?
> Otieno: Tutakula nyama choma pale ziwani. | We'll eat nyama choma there at the lake.
< John: Safi sana! Tutaonana Jumamosi. | Excellent! See you on Saturday.
  x: Safi sana! Tutaonana Jumapili. ; Safi sana! Tulionana Jumamosi.
> Otieno: Poa. Usisahau, saa kumi na mbili na nusu! | Cool. Don't forget — six thirty!
