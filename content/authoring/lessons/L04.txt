@lesson garden-workshop
title: Weeds, Hammers and Nails: Garden and Workshop
level: 4
index: 1
theme: home
category: home
culture: Many Nairobi homes have a small garden — a "bustani" for flowers, or a "shamba" for vegetables like sukuma and spinach — and an older gardener who knows every plant by name. Address an older man respectfully as "mzee". Kupanda means both "to plant" and "to climb / get on" (a matatu); context tells you which. A "fundi" is anyone skilled with their hands, and calling someone "fundi kweli" (a real fundi) is high praise.
start: Saturday morning. Mzee Ochieng, who looks after the garden of your block, is already at work with a hoe.
grammar: present-na, ko-location, adjective-agreement, possessives

level: 4
index: 2
theme: safari
category: social
culture: The Maasai Mara is Kenya's most famous game park (mbuga ya wanyama), and "safari" is a Swahili word: it simply means "journey". All animals take the same agreement as people, whatever their noun class: simba watatu (three lions), twiga ni mrefu (the giraffe is tall), tembo ni wakubwa (elephants are big).

@lesson school-visit
title: Back to School: A Visit to Mwalimu Akinyi
level: 4
index: 3
theme: school
category: people
culture: Kenyan schools run from 8 a.m., in uniforms (sare) of bright colours, and every child knows the head teacher as "mwalimu mkuu". Pupils sit national exams at the end of primary and secondary school, and parents talk about results constantly. Classes are counted, not named: darasa la nne is "class four". Teachers are addressed simply as "mwalimu" (teacher) — it's a title of respect.

title: Happy, Worried, Angry: Moods and Personalities
level: 4

theme: people
category: people
culture: Kenyans often describe feelings with "niko na…" — literally "I am with…": niko na furaha (I'm happy), niko na wasiwasi (I'm worried), niko na hasira (I'm angry). Describing people is just as common, and the words come in singular and plural pairs: mcheshi / wacheshi (a funny person / funny people), mvivu / wavivu (lazy). Calling someone "mpole" (gentle, calm) is a big compliment.
