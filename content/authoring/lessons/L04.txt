# Level 4 — past -li-, negative past -ku-, object infixes (-ni- -ku- -m(w)- -wa-), days & time words

@lesson uber-nairobi-3
title: Taking an Uber, Part 3: Stuck in the Jam
level: 4
theme: uber
category: transport
culture: The Uber is back — and so is Kamau, your driver from Parts 1 and 2. Monday-morning traffic on Waiyaki Way is legendary, and drivers know every shortcut (njia ya mkato) through the leafy back roads of Lavington. Small talk in a jam is normal and expected. This time the conversation is about what already happened: the weekend, the accident, the missed call. That's the past tense -li-, and its negative -ku-.
start: Monday, 7:20 a.m. You order an Uber to the office — and Kamau pulls up again.
grammar: past-li, negative-past, object-infixes
gl: njia ya mkato = "a shortcut" — lit. "a way of cutting" (from kukata).
gl: Lavington = Lavington — leafy Nairobi suburb with back roads
gl: gari mbili = "two cars" — Kenyan n/n agreement. || Sanifu: magari mawili (ji/ma class) #sanifu-noun-classes
gl: ziliharibika = "were damaged / broke down" — zi- (they, n/n) + -li- (past) + -haribika. Kenyan n/n agreement for gari. || Sanifu: yaliharibika (magari, ji/ma) #past-li
gl: hakuna mtu = "nobody" — lit. "there is no person"
gl: kwa Waiyaki Way = "on Waiyaki Way" — Kenyan use of kwa. || Sanifu: kwenye barabara ya Waiyaki #locative-ni
gl: wikendi yote = "the whole weekend"
gl: ilizima = "(it) switched off / died" — i- (it, n/n) + -li- (past) + -zima. #past-li
gl: kasoro = "minus / to" — saa mbili kasoro dakika tano = 7:55 #time-swahili

> Dereva: Aah, John! Ni wewe tena! Habari ya wikendi? | Ah, John! It's you again! How was the weekend?
< Mteja: Kamau! Nzuri sana. Nilienda Naivasha na marafiki. | Kamau! Very good. I went to Naivasha with friends.
  x: Kamau! Nzuri sana. Nitaenda Naivasha na marafiki. ; Kamau! Nzuri sana. Ulienda Naivasha na marafiki.
> Dereva: Safi! Mlifanya nini huko? | Great! What did you do there?
< Mteja: Tuliona viboko, na tulikula nyama choma. | We saw hippos, and we ate nyama choma.
  x: Tutaona viboko, na tutakula nyama choma. ; Tuliona viboko, na tulila nyama choma.
> Dereva: Wee! Mimi sikuenda popote. Nilifanya kazi wikendi yote. | Wow! I didn't go anywhere. I worked the whole weekend.
< Mteja: Pole sana. Leo kuna foleni mbaya sana! | So sorry. Today there's a really bad jam!
  x: Asante sana. Leo kuna foleni mbaya sana! ; Pole sana. Leo kuna foleni nzuri sana!
> Dereva: Ndiyo. Kulikuwa na ajali kwa Waiyaki Way asubuhi. | Yes. There was an accident on Waiyaki Way this morning.
< Mteja: Kweli? Sikuona habari. Watu waliumia? | Really? I didn't see the news. Did people get hurt?
  x: Kweli? Sioni habari. Watu waliumia? ; Kweli? Sikuona habari. Watu wanaumia?
> Dereva: Hapana, hakuna mtu aliumia. Lakini gari mbili ziliharibika. | No, nobody got hurt. But two cars were damaged.
  s: Hapana, hakuna mtu aliumia. Lakini magari mawili yaliharibika.
< Mteja: Tutachelewa? Nilimwambia boss nitafika saa mbili. | Will we be late? I told the boss I'd arrive at eight.
  x: Tutachelewa? Nilikuambia boss nitafika saa mbili. ; Tutachelewa? Nilimwambia boss nilifika saa mbili.
> Dereva: Usijali. Nitapita njia ya mkato, kupitia Lavington. | Don't worry. I'll take a shortcut, through Lavington.
< Mteja: Poa! Jana nilikupigia simu, lakini hukuchukua. | Cool! Yesterday I called you, but you didn't pick up.
  x: Poa! Jana ulinipigia simu, lakini hukuchukua. ; Poa! Jana nilikupigia simu, lakini sikuchukua.
> Dereva: Aah, pole! Simu yangu ilizima — betri iliisha. | Ah, sorry! My phone died — the battery ran out.
< Mteja: Hakuna shida. Nitakupigia tena kesho asubuhi. | No problem. I'll call you again tomorrow morning.
  x: Hakuna shida. Nilikupigia tena kesho asubuhi. ; Hakuna shida. Nitampigia tena kesho asubuhi.
> Dereva: Sawa. Na hii ndiyo ofisi yako. Saa mbili kasoro dakika tano! | Okay. And this is your office. Five to eight!

@lesson chemist-sick
title: Feeling Sick: At the Chemist
level: 4
theme: health
category: health
culture: For everyday illnesses, Kenyans go to the chemist (pharmacy) first — the pharmacist asks about your symptoms, and you walk out with medicine and clear instructions. "Pole!" (sorry) is the first thing anyone says when you're unwell — it expresses sympathy, not an apology. Body-part nouns are a lovely place to see the Kenyan n/n habit: "kichwa inauma" (my head hurts) is everyday speech, while Sanifu says "kichwa kinauma" because kichwa is ki/vi.
start: You've felt awful since yesterday. You walk into a chemist near your flat.
grammar: past-li, negative-past, object-infixes
gl: chemist = pharmacy — Kenyans say "chemist" for the shop
gl: Kichwa inauma = "My head hurts" — Kenyan n/n agreement. || Sanifu: Kichwa kinauma (kichwa is ki/vi) #sanifu-noun-classes
gl: inauma = "it hurts" — i- (it) + -na- + -uma (bite, hurt). #present-na
gl: kidogo = "a little"
gl: Kukohoa = "coughing"
gl: tangu = "since"
gl: mara tatu kwa siku = "three times a day"
gl: baada ya kula = "after eating"
gl: maji mengi = "a lot of water" — ji/ma agreement (maji is ji/ma). Kenyans also say "maji mingi".
gl: ukipata = "if you get" — u- + -ki- (if) + -pata. You'll meet -ki- properly at Level 8. #ki-po-ka
gl: Dawa hii = "this medicine"
gl: vidonge = tablets (plural of kidonge)
gl: homa = "fever / flu" — Kenyans also use it for a bad cold

< Mteja: Habari, daktari. Sijisikii vizuri. | Hello, doctor. I don't feel well.
  x: Habari, daktari. Najisikia vizuri sana. ; Habari, daktari. Hujisikii vizuri.
> Daktari: Pole sana. Ulianza kuumwa lini? | So sorry. When did you start feeling sick?
< Mteja: Nilianza jana usiku. Kichwa inauma sana. | I started last night. My head hurts a lot.
  s: Nilianza jana usiku. Kichwa kinauma sana.
  x: Nitaanza jana usiku. Kichwa inauma sana. ; Nilianza jana usiku. Kichwa inaumwa sana.
> Daktari: Una homa? Ulikohoa? | Do you have a fever? Did you cough?
< Mteja: Ndiyo, nina homa kidogo. Sikulala vizuri. | Yes, I have a little fever. I didn't sleep well.
  x: Ndiyo, nina homa kidogo. Silali vizuri. ; Ndiyo, sina homa kidogo. Sikulala vizuri.
> Daktari: Ulikula nini jana? | What did you eat yesterday?
< Mteja: Nilikula chapati tu. Sikula chakula kingi. | I only ate chapati. I didn't eat much food.
  s: Nilikula chapati tu. Sikula chakula kingi.
  x: Ninakula chapati tu. Sikula chakula kingi. ; Nilikula chapati tu. Sili chakula kingi.
> Daktari: Sawa. Nitakupa dawa ya homa. | Okay. I'll give you medicine for the fever.
< Mteja: Asante. Ninakunywa dawa hii namna gani? | Thanks. How do I take this medicine?
  x: Asante. Unakunywa dawa hii namna gani? ; Asante. Ninakula dawa hii wapi?
> Daktari: Kunywa vidonge viwili, mara tatu kwa siku, baada ya kula. | Take two tablets, three times a day, after eating.
< Mteja: Mara tatu kwa siku. Sawa, nimeelewa. | Three times a day. Okay, I've understood.
  x: Mara mbili kwa siku. Sawa, nimeelewa. ; Mara tatu kwa wiki. Sawa, nimeelewa.
> Daktari: Na kunywa maji mengi. Ukipata shida, rudi kesho. | And drink a lot of water. If you have trouble, come back tomorrow.
< Mteja: Sawa. Ni pesa ngapi? | Okay. How much is it?
  x: Sawa. Ni pesa wapi? ; Sawa. Ni pesa nani?
> Daktari: Ni mia nne. Pona haraka! | It's four hundred. Get well soon!

@lesson weekend-story
title: How Was Your Weekend?
level: 4
theme: friends
category: social
culture: Monday morning at a Kenyan office starts with "Wikendi ilikuwaje?" (how was the weekend?) — and a story. Telling what happened is all past tense: -li- for what you did, -ku- for what you didn't. Object infixes let you say who it happened to without extra words: nilimwona (I saw him/her), alinipigia (she called me), tuliwaona (we saw them).
start: Monday, 9 a.m. Your colleague Wanjiku is making tea in the office kitchen.
grammar: past-li, negative-past, object-infixes
gl: Wikendi ilikuwaje = "How was the weekend?" — i- (it) + -li- + -kuwa (be) + -je (how?). #past-li
gl: ilikuwaje = "how was it?" — i- (it) + -li- (past) + -kuwa (be) + -je (how?) #past-li
gl: ilikuwa = "it was" — i- (it) + -li- (past) + -kuwa (be). #past-li
gl: dadangu = "my sister" — dada + -angu, contracted. #possessives
gl: harusi = wedding
gl: hotel = hotel
gl: Mombasa = Mombasa — the coastal city
gl: shughuli nyingi = "lots of things to do / busy"
gl: tukacheza = "and then we played" — tu- + -ka- (and then) + -cheza. #ki-po-ka
gl: mpira = football
gl: Kwa nini = why?
gl: alinipigia = "she called me" — a- + -li- + -ni- (me) + -pigia. #object-infixes

> Wanjiku: Habari ya asubuhi, John! Wikendi ilikuwaje? | Good morning, John! How was the weekend?
< John: Ilikuwa nzuri sana! Nilienda harusi ya rafiki yangu. | It was really good! I went to my friend's wedding.
  x: Itakuwa nzuri sana! Nilienda harusi ya rafiki yangu. ; Ilikuwa nzuri sana! Nitaenda harusi ya rafiki yangu.
> Wanjiku: Wee! Harusi ilikuwa wapi? | Wow! Where was the wedding?
< John: Ilikuwa Karen. Watu wengi walikuja, na tulicheza muziki mpaka usiku. | It was in Karen. Lots of people came, and we danced until night.
  x: Ilikuwa Karen. Watu wengi walikuja, na tunacheza muziki mpaka usiku. ; Ilikuwa Karen. Watu wengi hawakuja, na tulicheza muziki mpaka usiku.
> Wanjiku: Safi sana! Ulimwona Baraka huko? | Great! Did you see Baraka there?
< John: Hapana, sikumwona. Hakuja. | No, I didn't see him. He didn't come.
  x: Hapana, sikukuona. Hakuja. ; Ndiyo, sikumwona. Hakuja.
> Wanjiku: Aah, nakumbuka! Alinipigia simu Jumamosi. Alienda Mombasa. | Ah, I remember! He called me on Saturday. He went to Mombasa.
< John: Kweli? Alienda kufanya nini? | Really? What did he go to do?
  x: Kweli? Ulienda kufanya nini? ; Kweli? Atakwenda kufanya nini?
> Wanjiku: Kazi tu. Na wewe, ulipumzika Jumapili? | Just work. And you, did you rest on Sunday?
< John: Hapana! Nilifua nguo, nikapika, halafu nilicheza mpira na majirani. | No! I did laundry, cooked, then played football with the neighbours.
  x: Hapana! Ninafua nguo, nikapika, halafu nilicheza mpira na majirani. ; Ndiyo! Nilifua nguo, nikapika, halafu nilicheza mpira na majirani.
> Wanjiku: Pole! Wikendi yako ilikuwa na shughuli nyingi. | Aww! Your weekend was busy.
< John: Ndiyo. Na wewe, ulifanya nini? | Yes. And you, what did you do?
  x: Ndiyo. Na wewe, utafanya nini? ; Ndiyo. Na wewe, nilifanya nini?
> Wanjiku: Mimi? Nililala tu! Sikutoka nyumbani hata kidogo. | Me? I just slept! I didn't leave the house at all.
