@lesson neighbours-greetings
title: Mambo, Neighbour! Greetings at the Gate
level: 1
index: 3
theme: greetings
category: social
culture: Kenyans greet before anything else — skipping the greeting and jumping straight to business comes across as rude. Who you greet decides how you greet: an older woman like your neighbour gets the polite "Habari ya asubuhi?" (and "mama" as a sign of respect), while her teenage son gets a casual "Mambo?" or "Niaje?". Women are often called by their first child's name — "Mama Njeri" is "Njeri's mum". You are John again, newly moved into a flat in Kilimani.
start: It's 7:30 on your first morning in the new flat. Mama Njeri from next door greets you at the gate.
grammar: greetings, ko-location, present-na
gl: Mama Njeri = "Njeri's mum" — Kenyan women are often called by their first child's name. Mama alone is a respectful way to address any older woman.
gl: namba tano = number five
gl: kazi njema = "have a good day at work" (lit. "good work")
gl: Tuonane baadaye = "see you later" — a set phrase (lit. "let's see each other later").
gl: Tuonane = "let's see each other" — set phrase used in goodbyes.
gl: kazini = "at / to work" — kazi + -ni (place suffix). #locative-ni

> Mama Njeri: Habari ya asubuhi? | Good morning? (lit. "News of the morning?")
< Mgeni: Nzuri sana, mama. Habari yako? | Very good, ma'am. How are you?
  x: Poa sana, boss! Niaje? ; Niko Kilimani.
> Mama Njeri: Salama. Wewe ni mgeni hapa? | Fine. Are you new here?
< Mgeni: Ndiyo. Ninaishi hapa, nyumba namba tano. | Yes. I live here, house number five.
> Mama Njeri: Karibu sana! Jina yako ni nani? | You're very welcome! What's your name?
  s: Karibu sana! Jina lako ni nani?
< Mgeni: Jina yangu ni John. Na wewe? | My name is John. And you?
  s: Jina langu ni John. Na wewe?
> Mama Njeri: Mimi ni Mama Njeri. Na Baraka ni mtoto wangu. | I'm Mama Njeri. And Baraka is my son.
> Baraka: Mambo? | What's up?
< Mgeni: Poa sana! Uko aje? | Very cool! How are you?
  x: Nzuri sana, mama. Habari yako? ; Poa sana! Niko aje?
> Baraka: Niko fiti. Unatoka wapi? | I'm good. Where are you from?
< Mgeni: Ninatoka Uganda, lakini ninafanya kazi hapa Nairobi. | I'm from Uganda, but I work here in Nairobi.
> Baraka: Aah, poa. Unaenda kazini sasa? | Ah, cool. Are you going to work now?
< Mgeni: Ndiyo, ninaenda kazini sasa hivi. | Yes, I'm going to work right now.
> Mama Njeri: Sawa. Kazi njema! | Okay. Have a good day at work!
< Mgeni: Asante, mama. Tuonane baadaye! | Thank you, ma'am. See you later!
  x: Karibu sana, mama! ; Asante, mama. Habari ya asubuhi?
> Baraka: Poa. Kwa heri! | Cool. Bye!

@vocab
Habari ya asubuhi? | Good morning? | Lit. "news of the morning?" — answer Nzuri. You'll also hear "Habari asubuhi?"
Nzuri | Good / fine | The standard reply to every "Habari…?" greeting.
Mambo? | What's up? | Casual — answer Poa. Lit. "matters?"
Uko aje? | How are you? | Casual Kenyan. Answer: Niko poa / Niko fiti.
Niko fiti | I'm fine | fiti comes from English "fit".
Wewe ni mgeni hapa? | Are you new here? | mgeni = guest / newcomer / stranger
Ninaishi… | I live… | From kuishi (to live). Ninaishi Kilimani.
Jina yangu ni… | My name is… | Everyday Kenyan form. | Jina langu ni… | Sanifu: jina is ji/ma class, so langu.
Unatoka wapi? | Where are you from? | Also: where are you coming from (just now)?
Ninafanya kazi… | I work… | Lit. "I am doing work". Ninafanya kazi Safaricom.
kazini | at / to work | kazi + -ni (the place suffix)
Kazi njema! | Have a good day at work! | Lit. "good work!"
Tuonane baadaye | See you later | Lit. "let's see each other later"
Kwa heri | Goodbye | To several people: Kwaherini.

@lesson intro-where-from
title: Nice to Meet You! Where Are You From?
level: 1
index: 4
theme: greetings
category: social
culture: At any Nairobi party or office, the first three questions are always the same: your name, where you're from, and what you do. Kenyans are curious about wazungu and other foreigners — why you came, how long you're staying, whether you like the food — and they're delighted by any attempt at Swahili. "Tuko pamoja" (we're together) is a warm Kenyan way of saying "I'm with you" — and also a casual goodbye.
start: Saturday evening at a friend's house party in Kileleshwa. A woman with a plate of samosas comes over to say hi.
grammar: question-words, present-na, ko-location, possessives
gl: Wanjiru = Wanjiru (a Kikuyu name)
gl: Yaya Centre = Yaya Centre — a shopping mall in Kilimani
gl: Kiingereza = English (the language)
gl: Kiswahili = Swahili (the language)
gl: Ninajifunza = "I'm learning" — ni- + -na- + -jifunza (learn).
gl: Unajifunza = "you're learning" — u- + -na- + -jifunza (learn).
gl: Ninafundisha = "I teach" — ni- + -na- + -fundisha (teach).
gl: pole pole = "slowly, little by little"
gl: Wakenya = Kenyans (one Kenyan = Mkenya)
gl: hali ya hewa = "the weather" — lit. "the state of the air"
gl: Tuko pamoja = "we're together" — Kenyan for "I'm with you"; also a friendly goodbye.
gl: kwa kazi = "for work"
gl: pamoja = together
gl: Hata mimi = "me too" — lit. "even me"

> Wanjiru: Mambo! Mimi ni Wanjiru. Wewe ni nani? | Hi! I'm Wanjiru. Who are you?
< John: Poa! Mimi ni John. | Cool! I'm John.
  x: Poa! Wewe ni John. ; Poa! Mimi ni Wanjiru.
> Wanjiru: Karibu, John. Unatoka wapi? | Welcome, John. Where are you from?
< John: Ninatoka Uganda, lakini ninaishi hapa Nairobi. | I'm from Uganda, but I live here in Nairobi.
  x: Unatoka Uganda, lakini unaishi hapa Nairobi. ; Ninaenda Uganda, lakini ninaishi hapa Nairobi.
> Wanjiru: Aah, poa! Unaishi wapi hapa Nairobi? | Oh, cool! Where in Nairobi do you live?
< John: Ninaishi Kilimani, karibu na Yaya Centre. | I live in Kilimani, near Yaya Centre.
  x: Anaishi Kilimani, karibu na Yaya Centre. ; Ninaishi Kilimani, karibu na Wanjiru.
> Wanjiru: Na unafanya kazi gani? | And what work do you do?
< John: Mimi ni mwalimu. Ninafundisha Kiingereza. | I'm a teacher. I teach English.
  x: Mimi ni mwalimu. Ninakula Kiingereza. ; Wewe ni mwalimu. Unafundisha Kiingereza.
> Wanjiru: Safi sana! Na Kiswahili je? Unajifunza Kiswahili? | Great! And Swahili? Are you learning Swahili?
< John: Ndiyo, ninajifunza pole pole. | Yes, I'm learning little by little.
  x: Ndiyo, unajifunza pole pole. ; Ndiyo, ninafundisha pole pole.
> Wanjiru: Unaongea vizuri! Uko Kenya kwa kazi? | You speak well! Are you in Kenya for work?
< John: Ndiyo, kwa kazi. Na pia ninapenda Kenya sana. | Yes, for work. And I also like Kenya a lot.
  x: Ndiyo, kwa kazi. Na pia unapenda Kenya sana. ; Ndiyo, kwa kazi. Na pia ninaenda Kenya sana.
> Wanjiru: Asante! Wakenya wanapenda wageni. Unapenda nini hapa? | Thank you! Kenyans love guests. What do you like here?
< John: Ninapenda watu, chakula na hali ya hewa. | I like the people, the food and the weather.
  x: Wanapenda watu, chakula na hali ya hewa. ; Ninapenda wapi, chakula na hali ya hewa.
> Wanjiru: Hata mimi! Na familia yako iko wapi? | Me too! And where is your family?
< John: Familia yangu iko Kampala. Mke wangu ni mwalimu pia. | My family is in Kampala. My wife is a teacher too.
  x: Familia yako iko Kampala. Mke wako ni mwalimu pia. ; Familia yangu ni Kampala. Mke wangu ni mwalimu pia.
> Wanjiru: Poa sana. Karibu Kenya, John! | Very cool. Welcome to Kenya, John!
< John: Asante sana, Wanjiru. Tuko pamoja! | Thanks a lot, Wanjiru. I'm with you!
  x: Karibu sana, Wanjiru. Tuko pamoja! ; Asante sana, Wanjiru. Niko wapi!
