# Level 8 — conditionals, -ki-/-po-/-ka-, relatives, habits.

@lesson market-marikiti-regular
title: A Regular at Marikiti Market
level: 8
index: 1
theme: market
category: commerce
culture: Marikiti is Nairobi's huge wholesale fruit and vegetable market. Become a regular and the sellers start to know you — and the prices get better. Habits are where Kenyan Swahili gets colourful: Sanifu uses hu- ("mimi hununua hapa" = I usually buy here), while Kenyans love adding -nga, borrowed from Luhya: "ninanunuanga hapa", "huwa nanunuanga". Deals are made with -ki- (if): "ukinunua kilo tatu, nitakupunguzia" (if you buy three kilos, I'll knock the price down for you).
start: Saturday morning at Marikiti. You walk up to the stall of Mama Wanjiru, whom you've bought from before.
grammar: habitual-hu-nga, ki-po-ka, relatives, conditional-nge
gl: Marikiti = Marikiti — Nairobi's wholesale fruit & veg market
gl: Mama Wanjiru = "Wanjiru's mum" — the seller
gl: Nilipofika = "When I arrived" — ni- + -li- + -po- (when) + -fika #ki-po-ka
gl: nikakutafuta = "and I looked for you" — ni- + -ka- (and then) + -ku- + -tafuta #ki-po-ka
gl: hununua = "(you) usually buy" — hu- (habitual) + -nunua #habitual-hu-nga
gl: Huwa = "usually / tends to be" — hu- + -wa (be): sets up a habit. #habitual-hu-nga
gl: ninanunuanga = "I usually buy" — Kenyan -nga habitual, from Luhya #habitual-hu-nga

@lesson landlord-leak
title: The Landlord and the Leaking Roof
level: 8
index: 2
theme: home
category: home
culture: Most Nairobians rent (kupanga nyumba), and the long rains (March–May) are when roofs, gutters and landlords get tested. Complaints in Kenya are made politely but firmly — and conditionals are your best tool: "Kama ungalimleta fundi mapema…" (if you had brought a fundi earlier…). In everyday speech Kenyans use -nge- for both present and past conditions ("Ungemleta fundi, hii haingetokea"), while Sanifu keeps -ngali- for the past. Rent is kodi.
start: After a night of heavy rain, your bedroom ceiling is dripping again. You call your landlord, Bwana Otieno.
grammar: conditional-nge, relatives, ki-po-ka

@lesson hospital-visit
title: At the Hospital
level: 8
index: 3
theme: health
category: health
culture: For anything beyond the chemist, Kenyans head to a hospital or clinic: you register at reception, see a nurse for "vitals", then the doctor. Doctors ask about habits ("huwa unakunywa maji ya kutosha?" — do you usually drink enough water?) and give if/when instructions with -ki-: "ukiona homa inapanda, rudi" (if you see the fever rising, come back). Relatives describe what happened: "dawa ambayo nilipewa" (the medicine that I was given).

culture: Thomson's Falls (Maporomoko ya Thomson) plunge 74 metres off the Aberdare range at Nyahururu, one of Kenya's highest towns, surrounded by tea and potato farms.
