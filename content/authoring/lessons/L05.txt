@lesson market-maasai-bargain
title: Bargaining at the Maasai Market
level: 5
theme: market
category: commerce
culture: The Maasai Market moves around Nairobi through the week — rows of baskets (vikapu), beaded jewellery, kikoi cloths and carvings. The first price is for tourists; bargaining is expected and friendly. The rhythm: show interest, compare ("this one is nicer than that one"), name a price, and settle with "Tumepatana!" (we've agreed). The -me- tense is everywhere in a deal: "nimeona" (I've seen), "umepunguza" (you've lowered it), "nimeamua" (I've decided).
start: Saturday afternoon at the Maasai Market. A seller waves you over to her baskets.
grammar: perfect-me, perfect-negative-ja, comparatives, adjective-agreement
gl: kikapu = "basket" — ki/vi noun (plural vikapu). Kenyans say "kikapu hii". || Sanifu: kikapu hiki (ki/vi) #sanifu-noun-classes
gl: kikapu hii = "this basket" — Kenyan n/n demonstrative. || Sanifu: kikapu hiki #sanifu-noun-classes
gl: kikapu ile = "that basket" — Kenyan. || Sanifu: kikapu kile #sanifu-noun-classes
gl: vikapu = baskets (plural of kikapu)
gl: elfu mbili = "two thousand"
gl: elfu moja = "one thousand"
gl: mia tano = "five hundred"
gl: elfu moja mia tano = "one thousand five hundred" — mia (hundreds) follows elfu (thousands) directly.
gl: Wee = "Hey! / Wow!" — surprise #discourse-particles
gl: bei ya mzungu = "the foreigner price" — said with a smile when the first price is too high.
gl: mkono = "by hand / hand" — kwa mkono = by hand
gl: Tumepatana = "We've agreed (on a price)!" — tu- + -me- + -patana (agree, reciprocal). #perfect-me
gl: shanga = beads

> Muuzaji: Karibu, sister! Angalia vikapu hivi. Ni vizuri sana. | Welcome, sister! Look at these baskets. They're very good.
  s: Karibu, dada! Angalia vikapu hivi. Ni vizuri sana.
< Mteja: Asante. Nimeona kikapu hii. Ni bei gani? | Thanks. I've seen this basket. How much is it?
  s: Asante. Nimeona kikapu hiki. Ni bei gani?
  x: Asante. Nitaona kikapu hii. Ni bei gani? ; Asante. Sijaona kikapu hii. Ni bei gani?
> Muuzaji: Hii ni elfu mbili tu. Imetengenezwa kwa mkono. | This one is just two thousand. It's been made by hand.
< Mteja: Wee! Elfu mbili? Hiyo ni bei ya mzungu! | Wow! Two thousand? That's the foreigner price!
  x: Wee! Elfu mbili? Hiyo ni bei nzuri sana! ; Asante! Elfu mbili? Hiyo ni bei ya mzungu!
> Muuzaji: Hapana! Kikapu hii ni kubwa kuliko ile, na ni nzuri zaidi. | No! This basket is bigger than that one, and it's nicer.
  s: Hapana! Kikapu hiki ni kikubwa kuliko kile, na ni kizuri zaidi.
< Mteja: Ni kweli, lakini sijawahi kulipa elfu mbili kwa kikapu. | That's true, but I've never paid two thousand for a basket.
  x: Ni kweli, lakini nimewahi kulipa elfu mbili kwa kikapu. ; Ni kweli, lakini sijawahi kulipa elfu mbili kwa nyumba.
> Muuzaji: Sawa. Kwa wewe, elfu moja mia tano. | Okay. For you, one thousand five hundred.
< Mteja: Bado ni ghali. Nitalipa elfu moja. | It's still expensive. I'll pay one thousand.
  x: Bado ni rahisi. Nitalipa elfu moja. ; Bado ni ghali. Nililipa elfu moja.
> Muuzaji: Aiii, umepunguza sana! Bei ya mwisho, elfu moja mia mbili. | Aiii, you've cut it a lot! Final price, one thousand two hundred.
< Mteja: Sawa. Na kama nitanunua vikapu viwili? | Okay. And if I buy two baskets?
  x: Sawa. Na kama nitanunua vikapu wawili? ; Sawa. Na kama nilinunua vikapu viwili?
> Muuzaji: Viwili? Basi, elfu mbili kwa vyote. Hiyo ni bei nzuri kuliko zote! | Two? Then two thousand for both. That's the best price of all!
< Mteja: Tumepatana! Nimeamua — nitachukua hii nyekundu na ile ya kijani. | Deal! I've decided — I'll take this red one and that green one.
  x: Hatujapatana! Nimeamua — nitachukua hii nyekundu na ile ya kijani. ; Tumepatana! Sijaamua — nitachukua hii nyekundu na ile ya kijani.
> Muuzaji: Asante sana, sister! Umechagua vizuri. Karibu tena! | Thank you so much, sister! You've chosen well. Come again!

@lesson restaurant-reservation
title: The Lost Reservation
level: 5
theme: food
category: food-drink
culture: Booking a table (kuweka meza / reservation) is common for dinners out in Nairobi. When something goes wrong, Kenyan service staff will smooth it over with "Pole kwa usumbufu" (sorry for the trouble) and a bit of patience on both sides. The -me- and -ja- tenses carry this whole scene: "nimefika" (I've arrived), "sijaona jina lako" (I haven't seen your name yet), "imeisha" (it's finished — sold out).
start: You arrive at a restaurant in Westlands for dinner. You booked a table yesterday — or did you?
grammar: perfect-me, perfect-negative-ja, comparatives
gl: Niliweka meza = "I reserved a table" — lit. "I put a table". #past-li
gl: kwa jina = "under the name"
gl: orodha = list
gl: Ngoja kidogo = "Wait a moment"
gl: niangalie = "let me look" — ni- + -angali- + -e (subjunctive). #subjunctive
gl: usumbufu = inconvenience / trouble
gl: Pole kwa usumbufu = "Sorry for the trouble"
gl: tofauti = different
gl: kosa = mistake
gl: imeandikwa = "it has been written" — i- + -me- + -andikwa (passive). #perfect-me
gl: menyu = menu
gl: kuku wa kukaanga = fried chicken
gl: samaki wa kuchoma = grilled fish
gl: inachukua = "it takes" (time)
gl: Nipe samaki = "Give me the fish"
gl: juisi ya maembe = mango juice
gl: ya nanasi = pineapple (juice)
gl: badala yake = "instead (of it)"

< Mgeni: Habari! Nimefika. Niliweka meza jana kwa jina Omondi. | Hello! I've arrived. I reserved a table yesterday under the name Omondi.
  x: Habari! Sijafika. Niliweka meza jana kwa jina Omondi. ; Habari! Nimefika. Nitaweka meza jana kwa jina Omondi.
> Mhudumu: Karibu! Ngoja kidogo, niangalie kwenye orodha. | Welcome! Wait a moment, let me check the list.
> Mhudumu: Samahani, sijaona jina lako kwenye orodha. | Sorry, I haven't seen your name on the list.
< Mgeni: Kweli? Nimepata ujumbe kwa simu jana. Hii hapa. | Really? I got a message on my phone yesterday. Here it is.
  x: Kweli? Sijapata ujumbe kwa simu jana. Hii hapa. ; Kweli? Umepata ujumbe kwa simu jana. Hii hapa.
> Mhudumu: Aah, nimeona! Meza imeandikwa kwa jina tofauti. Pole kwa usumbufu. | Ah, I see! The table was written under a different name. Sorry for the trouble.
< Mgeni: Hakuna shida. Meza iko tayari? | No problem. Is the table ready?
  x: Hakuna shida. Meza ziko tayari? ; Hakuna shida. Meza yuko tayari?
> Mhudumu: Ndiyo. Karibu, kaa hapa. Hii ni menyu. | Yes. Welcome, sit here. Here's the menu.
< Mgeni: Asante. Samaki ni tamu kuliko kuku hapa? | Thanks. Is the fish tastier than the chicken here?
  x: Asante. Samaki ni tamu zaidi kuku hapa? ; Asante. Samaki ni tamu sana kuku hapa?
> Mhudumu: Wateja wengi wanasema samaki ni bora zaidi. Lakini kuku inachukua muda mfupi. | Most customers say the fish is the best. But the chicken takes less time.
< Mgeni: Sijala tangu asubuhi. Lakini nitangoja — nipe samaki wa kuchoma. | I haven't eaten since morning. But I'll wait — give me the grilled fish.
  x: Nimekula tangu asubuhi. Lakini nitangoja — nipe samaki wa kuchoma. ; Sijala tangu asubuhi. Lakini sitangoja — nipe samaki wa kuchoma.
> Mhudumu: Sawa. Na cha kunywa? | Okay. And something to drink?
< Mgeni: Juisi ya maembe, tafadhali. | Mango juice, please.
  x: Juisi ya maembe, asante sana. ; Juisi ya maji, tafadhali.
> Mhudumu: Samahani, juisi ya maembe imeisha. Unataka ya nanasi badala yake? | Sorry, the mango juice is finished. Do you want pineapple instead?
< Mgeni: Imeisha? Haya, sawa. Nipe ya nanasi. | It's finished? Alright, fine. Give me pineapple.
  x: Haijaisha? Haya, sawa. Nipe ya nanasi. ; Imeisha? Haya, sawa. Nimpe ya nanasi.
> Mhudumu: Sawa. Chakula chako kitakuja hivi karibuni. | Okay. Your food will come soon.

@lesson new-colleague
title: The New Colleague
level: 5
theme: people
category: people
culture: Describing people is where Swahili agreement really shows: adjectives take the class of the person, so "tall" is mrefu for one person and warefu for several — while for things Kenyans reach for the n/n form (ndefu, nzuri). It's also where Kenyan small talk lives: who's new, who has arrived, who has ever worked where. "-wahi" turns any verb into "ever / never": amewahi kufanya kazi Kampala? (has she ever worked in Kampala?).
start: Your team is getting a new member today. Your colleague Juma stops by your desk.
grammar: adjective-agreement, perfect-me, perfect-negative-ja, wahi-ever-never, comparatives
gl: mfanyikazi mpya = "a new worker / employee" — m/wa agreement #adjective-agreement
gl: Faith = Faith (name)
gl: anaonekana = "she seems / she looks" — a- + -na- + -onekana. #present-na
gl: mkarimu = "generous / kind" — m/wa singular #adjective-agreement
gl: mpole = "calm / gentle" — m/wa singular #adjective-agreement
gl: mrefu = "tall" — m/wa singular #adjective-agreement
gl: mfupi = "short" — m/wa singular #adjective-agreement
gl: wafanyakazi = workers / staff
gl: uzoefu = experience
gl: idara = department
gl: ofisi hii = this office
gl: zaidi ya = "more than"
gl: miaka mitano = "five years" — m/mi agreement (miaka is m/mi in Sanifu) #sanifu-noun-classes

> Juma: John, umemwona mfanyikazi mpya? | John, have you seen the new colleague?
< John: Bado sijamwona. Amefika? | I haven't seen her yet. Has she arrived?
  x: Bado nimemwona. Amefika? ; Bado sijamwona. Nimefika?
> Juma: Ndiyo, amefika sasa hivi. Anaitwa Faith. | Yes, she's just arrived. She's called Faith.
< John: Faith ni mtu wa aina gani? | What kind of person is Faith?
  x: Faith ni kitu wa aina gani? ; Faith ni mtu wa aina wapi?
> Juma: Anaonekana mkarimu na mpole. Ni mrefu kuliko mimi! | She seems kind and calm. She's taller than me!
< John: Wee! Wewe ni mrefu sana, Juma! | Wow! You're very tall, Juma!
  x: Wee! Wewe ni ndefu sana, Juma! ; Wee! Wewe ni warefu sana, Juma!
> Juma: Haha, ndiyo. Na amewahi kufanya kazi Kampala. | Haha, yes. And she has worked in Kampala before.
< John: Kweli? Mimi pia nimewahi kuishi Kampala! | Really? I've lived in Kampala too!
  x: Kweli? Mimi pia sijawahi kuishi Kampala! ; Kweli? Mimi pia nimewahi kuishi Kampala jana!
> Juma: Basi mtaelewana! Ana uzoefu wa zaidi ya miaka mitano. | Then you'll get along! She has more than five years of experience.
< John: Safi. Wafanyakazi wengi hapa ni wapya, sivyo? | Great. A lot of the staff here are new, aren't they?
  x: Safi. Wafanyakazi wengi hapa ni mpya, sivyo? ; Safi. Wafanyakazi mengi hapa ni wapya, sivyo?
> Juma: Ndiyo. Idara yetu imekua sana mwaka huu. | Yes. Our department has grown a lot this year.
  s: Ndiyo. Idara yetu imekua sana mwaka huu.
< John: Nitaenda kumsalimia sasa. Yuko wapi? | I'll go and greet her now. Where is she?
  x: Nitaenda kukusalimia sasa. Yuko wapi? ; Nitaenda kumsalimia sasa. Iko wapi?
> Juma: Yuko pale, karibu na dirisha. Yule mwenye nguo nyeupe. | She's over there, by the window. The one in white clothes.
