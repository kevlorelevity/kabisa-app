# Level 7 — subjunctive & polite requests, verb extensions, Kenyan discourse particles.

@lesson petrol-station
title: Fill It Up: At the Petrol Station
level: 7
index: 1
theme: car
category: transport
culture: Kenyan petrol stations are full-service: an attendant fills the tank, checks the tyres, and washes the windscreen while you stay in the car. You ask for fuel by amount, not litres ("weka ya elfu tatu" = put in three thousand's worth). Polite requests use the subjunctive: "uweke" (would you put), "uangalie" (would you check), "nikulipe" (shall I pay you). "Naomba" (I'd like / may I have) is the classic polite opener.
start: You pull into a petrol station on Ngong Road. An attendant walks up to your window.
grammar: subjunctive, polite-requests, object-infixes
gl: Weka = "Put (in)!" — imperative of kuweka
gl: ya elfu tatu = "(fuel) for three thousand"
gl: Naomba = "May I have / I'd like" — the politest everyday opener. Lit. "I ask for". #polite-requests
gl: uweke = "(could you) put" — u- + -wek- + -e: subjunctive = polite request. #subjunctive
gl: uangalie = "(could you) check" — u- + -angali- + -e #subjunctive
gl: upepo = "air" (lit. "wind") — tyres are pumped with upepo
gl: tairi = tyre (plural matairi)
gl: ya nyuma = "the back one"
gl: Ebu = "Let me…" / "Come on, let's…" — softens a request #discourse-particles
gl: nione = "(let me) see" — ni- + -on- + -e #subjunctive
gl: nikulipe = "shall I pay you?" — ni- + -ku- (you) + -lip- + -e #subjunctive
gl: Usijali = "Don't worry" — u- + -si- + -jali #subjunctive
gl: Usiwashe = "Don't switch on" — u- + -si- + -wash- + -e: negative command #subjunctive
gl: injini = engine
gl: ili = "so that" — always followed by the subjunctive #subjunctive
gl: tusafishe = "(so that) we clean" — tu- + -safish- + -e #subjunctive
gl: kioo = windscreen / glass / mirror
gl: risiti = receipt
gl: mafuta = fuel / petrol / oil

> Mhudumu: Karibu, boss! Weka ya ngapi? | Welcome, boss! How much shall I put in?
< Dereva: Naomba uweke ya elfu tatu, tafadhali. | Please put in three thousand's worth.
  x: Naomba niweke ya elfu tatu, tafadhali. ; Naomba uweke ya elfu tatu, asante sana.
> Mhudumu: Sawa. Petroli au dizeli? | Okay. Petrol or diesel?
< Dereva: Petroli. Na uangalie upepo kwa matairi pia. | Petrol. And check the air in the tyres too.
  x: Petroli. Na niangalie upepo kwa matairi pia. ; Petroli. Na uangalia upepo kwa matairi pia.
> Mhudumu: Hakuna shida. Usiwashe injini, tafadhali. | No problem. Please don't start the engine.
< Dereva: Sawa. Na tairi ya nyuma — ebu nione. Iko sawa? | Okay. And the back tyre — let me see. Is it okay?
  x: Sawa. Na tairi ya nyuma — ebu uone. Iko sawa? ; Sawa. Na tairi ya nyuma — ebu nione. Iko wapi?
> Mhudumu: Haina upepo wa kutosha. Nitaongeza. | It doesn't have enough air. I'll add some.
< Dereva: Asante. Ufute kioo pia, ili nione barabara vizuri. | Thanks. Wipe the windscreen too, so that I can see the road well.
  x: Asante. Ufute kioo pia, ili unaona barabara vizuri. ; Asante. Nifute kioo pia, ili nione barabara vizuri.
> Mhudumu: Sawa sawa. Kioo ni chafu sana! | Sure thing. The windscreen is really dirty!
< Dereva: Ndiyo, mvua ilinyesha jana. Nikulipe kwa M-Pesa au kadi? | Yes, it rained yesterday. Shall I pay you by M-Pesa or card?
  x: Ndiyo, mvua ilinyesha jana. Unilipe kwa M-Pesa au kadi? ; Ndiyo, mvua inanyesha jana. Nikulipe kwa M-Pesa au kadi?
> Mhudumu: Yoyote. Lakini lazima uchukue risiti kwa ofisi. | Either. But you must get the receipt at the office.
< Dereva: Sawa. Ebu nilipe kwa M-Pesa, ni rahisi. | Okay. Let me pay with M-Pesa, it's easier.
  x: Sawa. Ebu ulipe kwa M-Pesa, ni rahisi. ; Sawa. Ebu nilipa kwa M-Pesa, ni rahisi.
> Mhudumu: Poa. Usijali, nitakuletea risiti. Safari njema! | Cool. Don't worry, I'll bring you the receipt. Safe travels!

@lesson car-breakdown
title: Car Trouble on Mombasa Road
level: 7
index: 2
theme: car
category: transport
culture: Breaking down is a shared Kenyan experience — strangers stop, someone knows a mechanic (fundi), and a tow truck appears faster than you'd expect. Kenyans treat gari (car) as n/n: "gari yangu imeharibika" (my car has broken down), where Sanifu says "gari langu limeharibika". The subjunctive shows up whenever you ask, suggest or arrange: "nimpigie fundi?" (shall I call a mechanic?), "tungoje hapa" (let's wait here).
start: Your car stalls on the shoulder of Mombasa Road. A passing motorist, Mwangi, pulls over.
grammar: subjunctive, polite-requests, perfect-me, helper-verbs
gl: gari yako = "your car" — Kenyan n/n agreement. || Sanifu: gari lako (ji/ma) #sanifu-noun-classes
gl: imeharibika = "has broken down" — i- (it, n/n) + -me- + -haribika. Kenyan agreement for gari. || Sanifu: limeharibika #perfect-me
gl: ghafla = suddenly
gl: ikazima = "and it stalled" — i- + -ka- (and then) + -zima #ki-po-ka
gl: Nimpigie = "Shall I call him?" — ni- + -m- (him) + -pigi- + -e #subjunctive
gl: fundi = mechanic / skilled repairman
gl: Tungoje = "Let's wait" — tu- + -ngoj- + -e (the final -a becomes -e) #subjunctive
gl: kivuli = shade
gl: Mwangi = Mwangi (name)
gl: aje = "(that) he comes" — a- + -j- + -e: subjunctive of kuja #subjunctive
gl: aivute = "(that) he tows it" — a- + -i- (it) + -vut- + -e #subjunctive
gl: mekanika = mechanic
gl: Ninakushukuru = "I'm grateful to you" — ni- + -na- + -ku- + -shukuru #object-infixes
gl: usinilipe = "don't pay me" — u- + -si- + -ni- (me) + -lip- + -e #subjunctive
gl: Utanilipa = "You'll pay me" #future-ta
gl: kwa kunisaidia = "for helping me"
gl: Mungu akubariki = "God bless you"
gl: kesho kutwa = the day after tomorrow

> Mwangi: Pole, boss! Gari yako imeharibika? | Sorry, boss! Has your car broken down?
  s: Pole, bwana! Gari lako limeharibika?
< Wewe: Ndiyo. Ilikuwa inaenda vizuri, halafu ghafla ikazima. | Yes. It was going fine, then suddenly it stalled.
  x: Ndiyo. Itakuwa inaenda vizuri, halafu ghafla ikazima. ; Hapana. Ilikuwa inaenda vizuri, halafu ghafla ikazima.
> Mwangi: Ebu nione. Hmm, sijui shida ni nini. Nimpigie fundi wangu? | Let me see. Hmm, I don't know what the problem is. Shall I call my mechanic?
< Wewe: Ndiyo, tafadhali. Mpigie! Sijui fundi yeyote hapa. | Yes, please. Call him! I don't know any mechanic here.
  x: Ndiyo, tafadhali. Nipigie! Sijui fundi yeyote hapa. ; Ndiyo, tafadhali. Mpigie! Najua fundi yeyote hapa.
> Mwangi: Sawa. Amesema atakuja baada ya dakika thelathini. | Okay. He says he'll come in thirty minutes.
< Wewe: Thelathini? Tungoje hapa, au twende kwenye kivuli? | Thirty? Should we wait here, or go into the shade?
  x: Thelathini? Tunangoja hapa, au twende kwenye kivuli? ; Thelathini? Tungoje hapa, au tuende kwenye maji?
> Mwangi: Twende kwenye kivuli. Jua ni kali. | Let's go into the shade. The sun is strong.
< Wewe: Na kama fundi hawezi kuitengeneza hapa? | And if the mechanic can't fix it here?
  x: Na kama fundi anaweza kuitengeneza hapa? ; Na kama fundi hawezi kunitengeneza hapa?
> Mwangi: Basi inabidi tumwambie aivute mpaka kwa mekanika. | Then we'll have to tell him to tow it to the mechanic.
< Wewe: Sawa. Naomba umwambie anipigie simu akifika. | Okay. Please tell him to call me when he arrives.
  x: Sawa. Naomba uniambie anipigie simu akifika. ; Sawa. Naomba umwambie nimpigie simu akifika.
> Mwangi: Hakuna shida. Usijali, Nairobi ni hivi tu. | No problem. Don't worry, that's just Nairobi.
< Wewe: Ninakushukuru sana kwa kunisaidia. Nikulipe kitu? | I'm very grateful to you for helping me. Shall I give you something?
  x: Ninakushukuru sana kwa kunisaidia. Unilipe kitu? ; Unanishukuru sana kwa kunisaidia. Nikulipe kitu?
> Mwangi: Hapana, usinilipe! Leo ni wewe, kesho ni mimi. | No, don't pay me! Today it's you, tomorrow it's me.

@lesson fundi-at-home
title: Instructions for the Fundi
level: 7
index: 3
theme: home
category: home
culture: In Kenya you don't call "a plumber" or "an electrician" — you call a fundi: fundi wa maji (plumber), fundi wa stima (electrician), fundi wa mbao (carpenter). Agreeing on the price before work starts is essential ("tuongee bei kwanza"). Giving instructions politely uses the subjunctive: "urekebishe" (please fix), "usiguse" (don't touch), "uniambie" (tell me). The everyday Kenyan word for electricity is stima; Sanifu prefers umeme.
start: Water is dripping from the bathroom ceiling. A fundi arrives at your flat.
grammar: subjunctive, polite-requests, perfect-me, object-infixes
gl: fundi wa maji = plumber — lit. "craftsman of water"
gl: bafu = bathroom
gl: bomba = pipe / tap

gl: imevunjika = "it's broken" — i- + -me- + -vunjika (stative) #perfect-me

gl: urekebishe = "(please) fix" — u- + -rekebish- + -e #subjunctive
gl: usiguse = "don't touch" — u- + -si- + -gus- + -e #subjunctive
gl: stima = electricity — everyday Kenyan word || Sanifu: umeme
gl: nizime = "(shall) I switch off" — ni- + -zim- + -e #subjunctive
gl: uniambie = "(please) tell me" — u- + -ni- + -ambi- + -e #subjunctive
gl: sakafu = floor
gl: isichafuke = "so that it doesn't get dirty" — i- + -si- + -chafuk- + -e #subjunctive

> Fundi: Ebu nione. Aah, bomba imevunjika. Inabidi niibadilishe. | Let me see. Ah, the pipe is broken. I'll have to replace it.

  x: Ni rahisi kidogo. Tuseme elfu moja na mia mbili? ; Ni ghali kidogo. Tunasema elfu moja na mia mbili?
> Fundi: Sawa, boss. Lakini usiguse stima ya bafu wakati ninafanya kazi. | Okay, boss. But don't touch the bathroom electricity while I'm working.
< Wewe: Nizime stima yote? | Shall I switch off all the electricity?
  x: Uzime stima yote? ; Nizima stima yote?
> Fundi: Ndiyo, uzime, ili tusiwe na shida. | Yes, switch it off, so that we don't have any trouble.
< Wewe: Sawa. Na tafadhali ufunike sakafu, isichafuke. | Okay. And please cover the floor so it doesn't get dirty.
  x: Sawa. Na tafadhali nifunike sakafu, isichafuke. ; Sawa. Na tafadhali ufunike sakafu, ichafuke.
> Fundi: Hakuna shida. Nitamaliza baada ya saa moja. | No problem. I'll finish in an hour.
< Wewe: Poa. Ukimaliza, uniambie. Nikupe chai? | Cool. When you finish, tell me. Shall I give you some tea?
  x: Poa. Ukimaliza, nikuambie. Nikupe chai? ; Poa. Ukimaliza, uniambie. Unipe chai?
> Fundi: Asante sana, boss. Chai ni sawa kabisa! | Thank you very much, boss. Tea is just perfect!

@lesson police-stop
title: Pulled Over: A Traffic Police Stop
level: 7
index: 4
theme: police
category: work-admin
culture: Traffic police stops are routine on Kenyan roads: the officer (address them as "afande") checks your licence, insurance sticker and the car's lights and tyres. Stay calm, polite and patient. You may hear a hint about "kitu kidogo" (something small) — a bribe; the safe, legal answer is to politely accept the official process (a fine or court date) and never offer money. Verb extensions run through every line: simamisha (make stop), nimesimamishwa (I've been stopped), umefanyiwa ukaguzi (you've had an inspection done on you).
