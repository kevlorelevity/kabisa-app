@lesson police-stop
title: Pulled Over: A Traffic Police Stop
level: 7
theme: police
category: work-admin
culture: Traffic police stops are routine on Kenyan roads: the officer (address them as "afande") checks your licence, insurance sticker and the car's lights and tyres. Stay calm, polite and patient. You may hear a hint about "kitu kidogo" (something small) — a bribe; the safe, legal answer is to politely accept the official process (a fine or court date) and never offer money. Verb extensions run through every line: simamisha (make stop), nimesimamishwa (I've been stopped), umefanyiwa ukaguzi (you've had an inspection done on you).
start: A traffic officer waves you to the side of the road near Westlands. You're driving.
grammar: verb-extensions, discourse-particles, past-li
gl: Simamisha = "Pull over / stop (the car)!" — simama (stop) + -isha (make): causative. #verb-extensions
gl: pembeni = at the side
gl: afande = "officer" — respectful address for police
gl: nimesimamisha = "I've stopped (the car)" — causative of kusimama #verb-extensions
gl: Naomba = "May I see / I'd like" — polite request #polite-requests
gl: leseni ya kuendesha = driving licence
gl: Hizi hapa = "Here they are"
gl: unajua = "do you know"
gl: kwa nini = why
gl: nimekusimamisha = "I've stopped you" — ni- + -me- + -ku- (you) + -simamisha #verb-extensions
gl: kwa kasi = fast / at speed
gl: Sikujua = "I didn't know"
gl: nilikuwa naenda = "I was going" — past continuous: nilikuwa + present. #continuous-tenses
gl: ukaguzi = inspection
gl: Umefanyiwa = "Have you had … done (on your car)?" — fanya + -i- (for) + -w- (passive): "has been done for you". #verb-extensions
gl: nilifanyiwa = "I had it done" #verb-extensions
gl: mwezi uliopita = last month
gl: Kwa hivyo = "So…" #discourse-particles
gl: Unaweza kujitetea = "Can you defend yourself?" — a well-known hint at a bribe. #helper-verbs
gl: faini = fine (penalty)
gl: rasmi = official
gl: risiti = receipt
gl: kumbe = "oh! so…" — surprise #discourse-particles
gl: Basi = "Well then / okay" #discourse-particles
gl: Endesha = "Drive!" #imperatives
gl: kwa uangalifu = carefully
gl: Nitazingatia = "I'll stick to / observe"
gl: sheria za barabarani = the rules of the road

  x: Asante sana, afande. Nilizingatia sheria za barabarani. ; Asante sana, afande. Utazingatia sheria za barabarani.

Nairobi's Uber drivers often keep regular customers' numbers and do favours — returning lost items, waiting, running small errands. The prepositional extension (-ia/-ea) is how you talk about favours: "nimekuletea" (I've brought it to you), "nikuwekee" (shall I keep it for you), "ulinisubiria" (you waited for me).

@lesson office-meeting
title: The Monday Meeting Mix-up
level: 7
theme: work
category: work-admin

Passive verbs are how office news travels: "mkutano umeahirishwa" (the meeting has been postponed), "ripoti iliandikwa na Faith" (the report was written by Faith), "tuliambiwa" (we were told). Reciprocals describe teamwork: tunasaidiana (we help each other), tujadiliane (let's discuss it together).
